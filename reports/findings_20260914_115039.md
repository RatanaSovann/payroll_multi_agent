**Dataset:** 900 pay run records, 150 employees

---

## Executive Summary

A compliance review of payroll data for the January–March 2026 period has identified three material findings across Super Guarantee (SG) obligations and PAYG withholding, representing a combined direct financial exposure of **$58,196.30** and an estimated ATO SGC penalty exposure of **$47,374**. In total, **47 employees** are affected across 245 discrete pay run breaches, and all three findings are rated **HIGH** risk. The most urgent priority is to audit and remediate payroll system SG calculation logic — in particular for the casual workforce — and lodge a voluntary disclosure with the ATO before penalties are assessed; this is especially time-critical given Payday Super takes effect **1 July 2026**, less than four months from now.

## Finding 1: Super Guarantee Underpayment — 🔴 HIGH

### Issue

The payroll system has failed to remit the correct SG amount for **33 employees** across **198 pay run instances**, producing a cumulative shortfall of **$37,899.42** over the six-period review window. The average shortfall per non-compliant pay run is **$191.41**. The scale and distribution of this finding — spanning all six departments and all eight states — indicates a systemic calculation or configuration defect rather than isolated administrative error.

### Regulatory Basis

- **Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Cth)**, ss. 19–23: Employers must contribute SG at the legislated rate (11.5% from 1 July 2024; rising to 12% from 1 July 2025) on ordinary time earnings for each eligible employee each quarter.
- Failure to meet the quarterly SG charge shortfall triggers the **Superannuation Guarantee Charge (SGC)**, which is non-deductible and calculated on total salary and wages (a broader base than ordinary time earnings), plus an interest component of **10% per annum** and an administration fee of **$20 per employee per quarter**.
- Voluntary disclosure prior to an ATO audit is the primary mechanism for penalty mitigation under the **Tax Administration Act 1953**.

## Finding 2: PAYG Withholding Inconsistency — 🔴 HIGH

### Issue

PAYG withholding amounts are materially inconsistent with ATO Schedule 1 tax tables for **14 employees** across **47 pay run instances**, generating total over- and under-withholding exposure of **$20,296.88**. The average deviation from the correct withholding amount is **27.6%**, with a maximum deviation of **39.9%**. Deviations of this magnitude are not attributable to rounding or timing; they indicate that withholding tables or employee tax file declarations are incorrectly configured in the payroll system.

### Regulatory Basis

- **Income Tax Assessment Act 1936 (Cth)**, s. 221C and **Taxation Administration Act 1953 (Cth)**, Schedule 1, Division 12: Employers are required to withhold amounts from salary and wages in accordance with ATO tax withholding schedules and remit those amounts to the ATO.
- Under-withholding creates primary tax liability for the employee but may expose the employer to **failure-to-withhold penalties** under s. 16-25 of Schedule 1.
- Over-withholding, while generating no direct ATO penalty, constitutes an unreasonable reduction in employee take-home pay and carries **employee relations and Fair Work risk**, particularly where it is persistent and systemic.

Under-withholding is the predominant issue (57% of flagged runs), representing the higher regulatory risk. Employees in these pay runs will face unexpected tax shortfalls at assessment — a significant employee welfare concern and potential reputational issue.

Note that the `payg_deviation_pct` field contained **24 null values** in the source data; the above figures are based on the 876 records where deviation was calculable (see Data Quality Notes).

## Finding 3: Missing Super — Casual Employees — 🔴 HIGH

### Issue

**16 casual employees** have received no super contributions — or contributions materially below the required SG rate — across **96 pay run instances**, producing a total shortfall of **$20,350.23**. This sub-population is fully captured within the Finding 1 SG total and is presented separately due to its distinct root cause and regulatory profile. The absence of contributions for casual employees is not attributable to a calculation error; it appears the payroll system is failing to trigger super calculations for a segment of the casual workforce entirely.

### Regulatory Basis

- **Superannuation Guarantee (Administration) Act 1992 (Cth)**, as amended effective **1 July 2022**: The previous $450 per month minimum earnings threshold for SG eligibility was abolished. All casual employees are now SG-eligible from the first dollar earned, regardless of hours worked or monthly earnings.
- There are no state-based exemptions or award-based carve-outs that would exclude these employees from entitlement.
- This represents an absolute compliance obligation — there is no discretionary element.

## Recommendations

1. **Immediate: Lodge a voluntary disclosure with the ATO** (within 14 days) covering the SG shortfall of $37,899.42 across 33 employees. Voluntary disclosure is the primary mechanism to reduce Part 7 penalties from up to 200% of SGC to nil. Engage your legal counsel or tax advisor to prepare the s. 264-1 disclosure package concurrently with remediation.

2. **Immediate: Remediate casual employee SG configuration** in the payroll system within 14 days. The five employees with shortfalls across all six pay periods (EMP0018, EMP0110, EMP0035, EMP0126, EMP0024) should be backpaid with super contributions plus 10% per annum interest effective immediately. Confirm with your super fund administrator that late contributions can be allocated to the correct periods.

3. **Within 30 days: Audit SG calculation logic for all employment types.** Engage the payroll system vendor to review the earnings base used for SG calculations — particularly for Finance (27.9% of total shortfall) and SA/ACT employees. Confirm that all allowances, bonuses, and overtime components are correctly included in ordinary time earnings as defined under the SGAA.

4. **Within 30 days: Reconfigure PAYG withholding tables.** IT department deviations of up to 39.9% require urgent investigation. Review all active Tax File Number declarations and withholding variation notices lodged by employees. Verify that the payroll system's withholding tables have been updated to reflect current ATO Schedule 1 rates. For employees who have been under-withheld, notify them promptly so they can arrange voluntary additional withholding or PAYG instalments to manage their year-end tax position.

5. **Within 30 days: Individually notify affected employees.** All 47 affected employees should receive written communication confirming the nature of the error, the remediation action being taken, and (for PAYG under-withholding) the potential personal tax implications. Document all communications for ATO disclosure purposes.

6. **Within 60 days: Resolve the 450 missing `pay_period_start` values** in the source payroll system. This data gap directly impairs SG timing validation and will constitute a critical deficiency under Payday Super's transaction-by-transaction settlement model. Identify whether this is a data export issue or a system recording failure, and implement validation controls to prevent recurrence.

7. **Within 90 days: Implement automated compliance controls.** Build pre-payment validation rules into the payroll system to flag any pay run where `super_paid < expected_sg * 99.9%` or `|payg_withheld − correct_payg| / correct_payg > 10%` before payment is authorised. These controls should generate exception reports reviewed by the Payroll Manager each period.

8. **Before 30 June 2026: Complete a Payday Super readiness assessment.** Engage your payroll system vendor and superannuation fund to confirm real-time payment capability is in place before the 1 July 2026 effective date (see Payday Super Readiness section below).

## Payday Super Readiness

The Treasury Laws Amendment (Better Targeted Superannuation and Other Measures) Act introduces **Payday Super**, effective **1 July 2026**, requiring employers to remit SG contributions on or before each employee's pay day (replacing the current quarterly remittance model). For this employer, the implications are significant and require immediate action.

The current review has identified systemic SG calculation failures affecting 22% of the workforce (33 of 150 employees). Under Payday Super, each of these failures would become a **per-pay-run breach** rather than a quarterly charge event — dramatically increasing both the frequency of SGC liability and the administrative burden of remediation. The 16 casual employees currently receiving no super at all would generate an SGC event **every fortnight**.

Additionally, the 450 missing `pay_period_start` values in the current dataset are directly incompatible with Payday Super's transaction-by-transaction audit trail requirements. The ATO will require employers to demonstrate that each super payment was made on or before the corresponding pay date — this is not achievable without complete and accurate pay period boundary data.

**Minimum actions required before 30 June 2026:**
- Resolve all SG calculation errors identified in this report
- Confirm payroll system can generate and transmit super payment instructions on each pay date
- Test integration with superannuation fund clearing house for same-day settlement
- Update employee data records to ensure fund details are current for all 150 employees
- Brief the Board or Audit Committee on Payday Super obligations and the remediation timeline

**Overall Payday Super readiness assessment: CRITICAL RISK.** This employer is not currently in a position to meet the 1 July 2026 requirements without material system and process remediation.

## Data Quality Notes

The following caveats from the data validation process should be considered when interpreting the findings above:

- **450 null `pay_period_start` values (50% of records):** The start date for every second pay record is missing from the source data export. All compliance findings in this report are based on `pay_date` and `pay_period_end` as proxies for period timing. This does not affect the quantum of the SG shortfall or PAYG deviation calculations (which rely on wage and withholding amounts rather than dates), but it does mean that **period-level timing analysis cannot be independently verified** from the dataset as provided. The source payroll system should be queried directly to confirm whether pay period start dates are recorded there.

- **24 null `payg_deviation_pct` values (2.7% of records):** PAYG deviation percentage was unavailable for 24 pay run records. These have been excluded from the PAYG deviation analysis. It is possible the 14-employee affected count and $20,296.88 exposure figure are slightly understated as a result. These records should be investigated individually.

- **Seven columns excluded from automated analysis:** The fields `period_number`, `pay_period_start`, `pay_period_end`, `sg_compliant`, `sg_shortfall`, `payg_deviation_pct`, and `payg_flagged` were unmapped in the initial schema validation pass. Compliance findings have been independently recomputed from source amounts (`super_paid` vs `expected_sg`; `payg_withheld` vs `correct_payg`) and are not dependent on the pre-computed flag fields. The independently computed findings are consistent with the flagged records in the source data.

- **One anomalous `pay_period_start` date detected:** Sample record EMP0001, Period 4 shows a `pay_period_start` of **2026-12-02** against a `pay_period_end` of 2026-02-25 — clearly a data entry error (December instead of February). This record has been treated on the basis of its `pay_period_end` and `pay_date` for analysis purposes but confirms that period date data integrity requires remediation at source.

- **Overall data quality rating: HIGH** (for wage, super, and PAYG amount fields). The compliance findings are considered reliable for the financial exposures reported. Period-level timing conclusions should be treated as indicative pending resolution of the `pay_period_start` issue.
